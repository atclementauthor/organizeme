OrganizeMe Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 17, 2026

OrganizeMe is a personal Android app built for and used by its developer to manage their own email, subscriptions, spam calls and photos. It is not sold or distributed to the public. This policy explains what data the app accesses and what it does with it.

Google user data the app accesses

OrganizeMe requests one Google permission: Google Drive, limited to files the app creates (https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.file). It uses this permission only to upload photos and videos from the user's phone into an "OrganizeMe Photos" folder in the user's own Google Drive, and to check that those uploaded copies are still present and intact before removing the originals from the phone. The app cannot see or change any other files in the user's Drive.

How data is used and stored

  • Photos and videos are sent directly from the phone to the user's Google Drive. They are not sent to any other server.
  • The app keeps a small record on the phone of which items have been backed up, so it does not upload them twice.
  • Email features connect directly from the phone to the user's email provider. Email passwords are stored only on the phone, encrypted with the Android Keystore.
  • Call screening runs entirely on the phone. Screened-call history never leaves the device.
  • The app has no servers, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking.

Sharing

OrganizeMe does not sell, share or transfer any user data to anyone. No data is used for advertising, and no humans read the data.

Limited Use

OrganizeMe's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Removing access and deleting data

  • Revoke Google Drive access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Uninstalling the app deletes everything it stored on the phone.
  • Backed-up photos remain in the user's Google Drive until the user deletes them.

Contact

Questions about this policy can be sent to the developer contact email shown on the app's Google sign-in screen.
